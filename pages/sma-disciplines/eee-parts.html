EEEE Parts

NASA's Electrical, Electronic, Electromechanical and Electro-optical (EEEE or Quad-E) Parts Program seeks to evaluate newly available and advanced electronic parts for NASA programs and projects, and maximize effectiveness and efficiency by collaborating with industry and other agencies.

The objectives include

  • Assessing the reliability of newly available electronic parts and packaging technologies for NASA projects through validations, assessments and characterizations, and the development of test methods and tools
  • Expediting infusion paths for advanced (emerging) electronic parts and packaging technologies by evaluating the readiness for manufacturability and project usage consideration
  • Providing NASA projects with technology selection, application and validation guidelines for electronic parts and packaging hardware and processes
  • Retaining and disseminating electronic parts and packaging assurance, reliability validations, tools, and availability information to the NASA community

NEPAG

NASA EEEE Parts Assurance Group

The role of NEPAG is to provide knowledge, tools, information and access to resources to enable the project parts engineers and parts specialists to optimally support the circuit designers and to promote processes that will exclude quality and reliability part failures from the advanced stages of the project life cycle.

NEPAG objectives are to

  • Establish an inter-agency working group of lead parts engineers from NASA centers for agency-wide coordination of parts issues
  • Develop information technology-based communication system and tools to increase efficiency
  • Create a knowledge base of part supplier quality
  • Develop assurance tools for Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) parts
    • Guidelines and procedures for qualification
    • Shared knowledge of qualification results
    • Knowledge base on current COTS industry products and trends
  • Provide support to the MIL system
  • Maintain a current NASA Electrical, Electronic, Electromechanical and Electro-optical (EEEE) parts selection list
  • Influence non-government standards bodies through active participation
  • Reduce incidence of EEEE parts failure

Susana P. Douglas

Electrical, Electronic, Electromechanical and Electro-optical (EEEE) Parts Manager and the NASA Electronic Parts and Packaging (NEPP) Deputy Program Manager 

Susana P. Douglas is currently serving as the Electrical, Electronic, Electromechanical and Electro-optical (EEEE) Parts Manager and the NASA Electronic Parts and Packaging (NEPP) Deputy Program Manager at Goddard Space Flight Center, with an overarching goal of continually increasing the Parts and Radiation Engineering capability for the Agency. Douglas also serves as Executive Chair for the NASA Supply Chain Security Working Group, an organization established to ensure collaboration of cross-Agency efforts to develop risk-based policies and procedures that promote the security of NASA supply chain processes. 

Douglas has served in various roles in electrical, electronic, electromechanical, and electro-optical (EEEE) parts engineering support for multiple missions at Goddard since joining the organization in 2010. She has served as lead parts engineer for various Goddard missions and more recently, has been actively involved in several key NASA-wide studies pertinent to the EEEE parts community led by the NASA Engineering and Safety Center (NESC), including “Recommendations on Use of Commercial-Off-The-Shelf (COTS) EEEE Parts for NASA Missions.” 

Douglas came to NASA with more than 10 years of failure analysis and materials characterization experience in the semiconductor industry, including root cause investigation of defect sources in product manufacturing lines. She holds a bachelor’s and master’s degree in materials science and engineering.

Shri Agarwal

NASA Electronic Parts Assurance Group Coordinator

Shri Agarwal is the NASA Electronic  Electronic Parts Assurance Group (NEPAG) coordinator, a role he assumed in February 2020. NEPAG is about sharing electronic parts failure and other experiences within NASA and with other domestic and international space organizations. Agarwal has supported the NASA Electronic Parts and Packaging program and NEPAG as the agency’s Point of Contact for microcircuits. 

Agarwal represents NASA on the Defense Logistics Agency’s Qualifying Activity for space microcircuits whose responsibilities are varied, including supply chain audits and infusion of new technology into military standards. He was instrumental in developing requirements for Class Y, a classification assigned to the advanced (system-on-a-chip complexity) ceramic-based non-hermetic microcircuits for space. He serves as the vice-chair of the Society of Automotive Engineers; Systems, Standards, and Technology Council; and Solid State Devices group and as chair of its Space subcommittee.

Based at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory/California Institute of Technology, Agarwal has received numerous awards, including NASA medals for his contributions to space flight projects. He has presented on matters related to space electronic parts at domestic and international meetings. He holds master’s degrees from the University of Southern California, Los Angeles; the Indian Institute of Technology, New Delhi, India; and Agra University, Agra, India. He is a member of the Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers and the Electrostatic Discharge Association.

Jonny Pellish

Electrical, Electronic and Electromechanical Parts Program Manager

Dr. Jonathan “Jonny” Pellish serves as NASA’s Electrical, Electronic and Electromechanical (EEE) Parts manager at Goddard Space Flight Center, responsible for workforce stewardship and coordinating agencywide discipline technical activities in the EEE Parts and radiation effects engineering communities. Pellish is also the acting manager for the NASA Electronic Parts and Packaging Program, which is operated by Goddard for the Office of Safety and Mission Assurance.

Prior to assuming his latest position, Pellish served on detail in the NASA Headquarters Office of the Chief Financial Officer Strategic Investments Division, as an associate chief in the Goddard Electrical Engineering Division, and as an associate head in the Flight Data Systems and Radiation Effects Branch, where he began his NASA career in 2008.

Pellish received a Bachelor of Science degree in physics from Vanderbilt University, Nashville, Tennessee, in 2004 and his Master of Science and doctorate degrees in electrical engineering from Vanderbilt University in 2006 and 2008, respectively. From 2004 to 2008, Pellish was a graduate research assistant in the Radiation Effects and Reliability Group in the Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at Vanderbilt University. From 2007 to 2008, Pellish held an IBM doctorate fellowship, which included a co-op position at the IBM Austin Research Laboratory. His research continues to focus on radiation effects and cross-cutting efforts for deep space exploration.

Pellish has authored or coauthored more than 60 refereed publications in addition to numerous conference and workshop presentations. He is a member of the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics and the Institute for Electrical and Electronics Engineers.

Peter Majewicz, Ph.D.

Electronic Parts and Packaging Program Manager

Dr. Peter Majewicz is the manager of the NASA Electronic Parts and Packaging (NEPP) program and is based at Goddard Space Flight Center.

The NEPP agencywide program evaluates new and emerging Electrical, Electronic, Electromagnetic and Electro-optical part technologies, shares technical knowledge, and develops tools for Electrical, Electronic, and Electromechanical (EEE) parts assurance. It generates recommendations about part performance, application, failure modes, test methods, Reliability, and Supply Chain Quality within the context of NASA space flight missions and hardware.

Previously, Majewicz supported the NEPP program as the Hybrid Working Group coordinator and managed the development of NASA-STD-8739.10, Electrical, Electronic, and Electromechanical (EEE) Parts Assurance Standard.

Majewicz spent five years as a NASA support contractor after retiring from active duty as a U.S. naval officer, ending a 22-year U.S. Navy career in 2009.

He earned a Master of Science in electrical engineering in 2005 from the Naval Postgraduate School in Monterey, California, and a doctorate in systems engineering in 2017 from George Washington University in Washington, D.C.

MIL-HDBK-978

NASA Parts Application Handbook (Volumes 1 through 5)
Print Version

The NASA Parts Application Handbook (MIL-STD-978) has been prepared to provide a source of technical information for NASA centers and NASA contractors and to maximize standard part usage. This handbook summarizes current technical knowledge over a broad spectrum of high reliability electrical and electronic component parts. The handbook will not only assist in resolving frequent problems involving component parts but will help avoid such problems by encouraging more knowledgeable part selection and application. This handbook is an integral part of the NASA Standard Parts Program with MIL-STD-975, the NASA Standard Electrical, Electronic and Electromechanical Parts List. This handbook should not be used to select specific parts since it may, for information purposes, describe technologies that aren't listed in MIL-STD-975. Specific parts should be selected from those shown in MIL-STD-975.

Parts and Materials Assessment (EEE, Mechanical, Parts Stress/Derating)

SMA-RM-WBT-411
Print Version

Application of proper parts and materials is the key to successful operation of a space or ground systems. Among many considerations when designing a system, reliability and availability of its parts are the foundational element of the overall performance of a system. Choosing the proper materials for a given set of environment (thermal cycles,  Electromagnetic Induction (EMI), off gassing, corrosion resistance, etc.) can be the difference between mission success and failure. Understanding how Electrical, Electronic, Electromechanical (EEE) and mechanical parts are chosen and applied in a system in what situation will be the final objective.

This course covers the requirements for parts (EEE and mechanical) and materials for use in space projects. The parts and material control program shall support the required mission life and environment. A design methodology that considers parts and materials is explained so that component reliability can be described. Parts and materials shall be selected and controlled based on performance, environmental criticality for safety and mission success, and lifetime requirements. This promotes safety and mission assurance processes that eliminate parts failure in the advanced phases of the life cycle. Some of the key topics are

  • Commercial Off-The-Shelf parts
  • Derating methods and principles
  • EMI/radiation concerns
  • Counterfeit part issues
  • Radiation hardness
  • Storage and age control
  • Risk evaluation

Course Number: SMA-OS-WBT-230

Length: 16.00 hours

Audience: This course is intended for engineers and engineering project managers.
